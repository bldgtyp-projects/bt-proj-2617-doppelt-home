For winter resiliency the **Phius REVIVE** protocol uses the [Standard Effective Temperature (SET)](https://bigladdersoftware.com/epx/docs/9-5/engineering-reference/resilience-metrics.html#set-hour) metric to judge
zone habitability during the stress-period. To comply with the **Phius REVIVE** standard, all zones must show:

- 0 hours below 2 °C [36 °F] **SET**
- < 216 °F-Hours below 12 °C [54 °F] **SET**

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <strong>Result: first criterion met, second missed narrowly</strong>
  <span>
    Every zone records **0 hours** below 2 °C SET, so the life-safety criterion clears with a large margin. The
    degree-hour criterion misses, but only just. Zones accumulate **219 to 241 °F-Hours** below 12 °C [54 °F] SET
    against the 216 °F-Hour allowance, a shortfall of between 1% and 12%. The lowest SET reached in any zone over the
    seven-day outage is 9.56 °C [49.2 °F], in the penthouse.
  </span>
</div>

#### What the plots show

Interior dry-bulb temperature falls from the 20 °C [68 °F] setpoint to a low of about **4.3 °C [39.8 °F]** by the
seventh day, while outdoor air drops to **-16.3 °C [2.7 °F]**. The decline runs close to monotonic, because this
building gets almost no winter solar gain to interrupt it. See
[Winter Radiation](/windows/#winter-radiation) for why.

The zones separate in the order the envelope predicts. The garden level holds up best, buffered by the ground and by
the surrounding masonry. The penthouse does worst, exposed on all sides and at the top of the stack. Best to worst
spans only about 22 °F-Hours, which says the building behaves as a single thermal mass and that improving any one
level in isolation will not do much.

#### What would close the gap

The shortfall is small enough that ordinary refinement of the design would likely close it, and several measures
recommended elsewhere in this report point the same way:

1. **Windows at U-<Var k="narrative.windows.comfort_target_u_value" /> installed rather than U-<Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" />.**
   Glazing is the largest single heat-loss path in this building, so it is the strongest lever here.
2. **Airtightness better than the <Var k="narrative.certification.ph_ach_limit" /> ACH50 target.** No control system
   regulates infiltration loss during an outage, so every improvement translates directly.
3. **Thermal-bridge resolution at the joist ends, the slab edge and the party-wall returns.** Already scheduled. The
   model assumes a 10% allowance that the detailing work has to earn.
4. **Freeze protection for plumbing.** At 4.3 °C [39.8 °F] interior, pipes in exterior wall cavities, in the penthouse
   and at the terrace level sit close to freezing. Keep plumbing runs inboard of the insulation line and off exterior
   walls. Decide this early and it costs nothing.

  <span>
    A small resilient heat source, such as a sealed-combustion or battery-backed unit serving one level, would move
    this result decisively. Given how close the building already sits to the threshold, we would treat that as a
    comfort and insurance call for the owner.
  </span>
</div>
