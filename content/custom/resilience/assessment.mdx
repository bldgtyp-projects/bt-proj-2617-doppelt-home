Alongside the whole-building annual-energy consumption model built in the
[Passive House Planning Package (PHPP)](https://passivehouse.com/en/home/planning-tools/), the home was assessed with
a detailed hourly model for 'resiliency' during two test-periods:

1. The home was simulated during a **summer heat-wave event** along with a power-outage which lasts for a full week.
2. The home was simulated during a **winter storm event** along with a power-outage which lasts for a full week.

These 'stress-test' simulations measure whether the building can hold safe and healthy interior conditions on
'Passive' measures alone, with all space conditioning equipment treated as inoperable. The test-periods follow the
[Phius REVIVE protocol](https://www.phius.org/phius-revive-2024), including its rules for occupancy, weather and
habitability thresholds. The REVIVE weather conditions shift the historical record toward a warmer future climate
rather than reproducing a past event.

- [Phius REVIVE 2024 Standard v24.1.1.pdf (7.7 MB)](https://www.phius.org/phius-revive-2024-standard-document)

Each simulation window runs for nine days: one day of normal operation before the event, the seven-day outage itself,
and one day of recovery after power is restored. The model splits into six zones, one for each occupied level:
**Garden**, **Parlor**, **Second**, **Third**, **Fourth**, and **Penthouse**. Results are reported per zone.

The envelope simulated is the **Recommended** package described in the rest of this report: R-20 interior insulation
at the masonry walls, R-60 at the roof, R-23 under the new basement slab, R-9 at the party wall,
<Var k="narrative.certification.ph_ach_limit" /> ACH50, and triple glazing at U-<Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" />
with a <Var k="narrative.user_defined.rec_window_shgc" /> SHGC.

#### How to read these results

Phius REVIVE is a stress test rather than a design-condition analysis. A building that fails it is still safe under
normal operation, and the recommended package delivers a large comfort and durability improvement over the existing
house. What the test establishes is what happens when the power is out for a week, which is worth knowing for a house
that will stand another century.
