The **Phius REVIVE** protocol uses the [Heat Index](https://bigladdersoftware.com/epx/docs/22-2/engineering-reference/resilience-metrics.html#heat-index) temperature to judge perceived thermal
habitability during the test-period. The results below plot the Heat Index for each zone across the full summer week.
Minimum compliance with the **Phius REVIVE** standard requires that the building demonstrate:

- 0-hours of **Heat Index** in the 'Extreme Danger' zone ( > 52 °C [125 °F] ).

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <strong>Result: does not comply</strong>
  <span>
Every zone breaks the 'Extreme Danger' threshold by a wide margin. Across the seven-day outage each level records
**29 to 30 hours** in 'Extreme Danger' against an allowance of zero, plus roughly 36 hours in 'Danger', 22 hours in
'Extreme Caution' and 56 hours in 'Caution'. Peak Heat Index reaches **55.7 °C [132.3 °F]** at the garden level and
stays above 54.8 °C [130.7 °F] in every other zone. The house does not stay habitable through a week-long summer
outage.
  </span>
</div>

#### Humidity drives this, not temperature

This is the part of the result that determines what to do about it.

Interior dry-bulb temperature peaks at **32.7 °C [90.9 °F]**, which on its own would put the house in the 'Extreme
Caution' band. Interior relative humidity is what pushes the Heat Index into 'Extreme Danger'. It sits between 94% and
97% at the peak and touches saturation several times during the week. The REVIVE weather file carries outdoor
dewpoints as high as 30 °C [86 °F] during the event, so ventilating the house, through open windows or through the
recovery ventilator, brings in air that is already near saturated. Occupant moisture then has nowhere to go once the
dehumidification equipment is off.

The envelope does its job. Masonry mass and insulation hold interior dry-bulb 2 to 6 °C below outdoor peaks and
flatten the daily swing, which is why the Heat Index falls back into the 'Caution' band each night. None of that
touches a latent-load problem.

#### What would actually change the result

1. **Backup power sized for dehumidification only.** A small battery or generator circuit serving a single
   dehumidifier, or the ventilation unit plus a dehumidifier, moves this result further than any envelope change.
   Latent removal is the binding constraint, and it needs electricity. This is what we would carry forward.
2. **Exterior shading at the penthouse and the fourth-floor terrace glazing.** It lowers the sensible peak, improves
   the daytime result, and is worth doing on its own merits. See [Summer Radiation](/windows/#summer-radiation).
3. **A designated refuge level.** The garden level runs the same Heat Index as the rest of the house here, but it has
   the most thermal mass and the least solar exposure, so a targeted measure pays off there first.
4. **Night flushing will not work.** Opening the house at night during this event pulls in air at a dewpoint above
   the interior dewpoint for much of the week. That strategy suits a dry climate and fails here.
