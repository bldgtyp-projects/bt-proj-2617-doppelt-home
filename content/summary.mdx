This report covers the design-phase energy modeling for the renovation of the Townhouse on West 11th Street, a landmarked
masonry row house in the Greenwich Village Historic District. The model describes all seven levels of the proposed
building, from the lowered basement through the new penthouse, and includes the rear extension and the rooftop penthouse. The model was built from the schematic drawing and CAD files provided on <Var k="source_files.cad_files_received_date" /> and
carries a treated floor area of <Var k="narrative.user_defined.icfa_ft2" /> ft<sup>2</sup> against
<Var k="narrative.user_defined.envelope_area_ft2" /> ft<sup>2</sup> of exposed envelope.

We tested five variants, from a code-minimum baseline through the full PHI EnerPHit energy-demand target. The findings
below drive the recommendations in the pages that follow.

#### What the model shows

1. **The envelope decides this building.** Moving from code-minimum assemblies to the recommended package cuts annual
   heating demand by more than half, from 96 to 44 kWh/m<sup>2</sup>-yr. Peak heating load drops from about 67,000
   Btu/h to about 35,000 Btu/h, and peak sensible cooling to roughly 1.3 tons. A single compact all-electric system
   can easily serve loads that small.

2. **Airtightness is the best-value measure available.** The baseline assumes 3.0 ACH50. The recommended target is
   <Var k="narrative.certification.ph_ach_limit" /> ACH50. On a building with
   <Var k="narrative.user_defined.envelope_area_ft2" /> ft<sup>2</sup> of envelope and embedded floor joists on three
   sides, hitting that costs drawing time and construction sequencing, and almost nothing in materials.

4. **Summer resilience is the one result that argues for improvements.** Under the Phius REVIVE seven-day summer
   outage, the modeled home does not stay habitable. See [Resilience](/resilience/) for the numbers and for what would
   move them.

#### What we recommend

- Target the **PHI "EnerPHit by Component"** variant as the design basis for the envelope and for schematic HVAC sizing.
- Carry <Var k="narrative.certification.ph_ach_limit" /> ACH50 as a contractual airtightness target, and test
  mid-construction rather than once at completion.
- Hold the window package at the modeled installed U-value of <Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" />
  Btu/hr-ft<sup>2</sup>-F or better. That clears the PHI thermal-comfort threshold of
  <Var k="narrative.windows.comfort_target_u_value" /> for this climate, which is what lets perimeter heating come
  out, and it clears the EnerPHit component limit of U-<Var k="narrative.certification.enph_uw_limit" /> as well.
- Plan the fresh-air system as a dedicated ERV with its own ducting, sized for roughly
  <Var k="narrative.user_defined.design_airflow_cfm" /> cfm at design.
- Add fixed or operable shading at the rear and penthouse glazing. Confirm the summer resilience result before the
  glazing package is fixed.
