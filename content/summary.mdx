This report covers the design-phase energy modeling for the gut renovation of 262 West 11th Street, a landmarked
masonry row house in the Greenwich Village Historic District. The model describes all seven levels of the proposed
building, from the lowered basement through the new penthouse, and includes the rear extension and the fourth-floor
terrace. It was built from the schematic drawing set issued <Var k="source_files.cad_files_received_date" /> and
carries a treated floor area of <Var k="narrative.user_defined.icfa_ft2" /> ft<sup>2</sup> against
<Var k="narrative.user_defined.envelope_area_ft2" /> ft<sup>2</sup> of thermal envelope.

We tested five variants, from a code-minimum baseline through the full PHI EnerPHit energy-demand target. The findings
below drive the recommendations in the pages that follow.

#### What the model shows

1. **The envelope decides this building.** Moving from code-minimum assemblies to the recommended package cuts annual
   heating demand roughly in half. Peak heating load drops from about 67,000 Btu/h to about 37,000 Btu/h, and peak
   sensible cooling to roughly 1.4 tons. Those numbers are what let a single compact all-electric system serve the
   whole house.

2. **Airtightness is the best-value measure available.** The baseline assumes 3.0 ACH50. The recommended target is
   <Var k="narrative.certification.ph_ach_limit" /> ACH50. On a building with
   <Var k="narrative.user_defined.envelope_area_ft2" /> ft<sup>2</sup> of envelope and embedded floor joists on three
   sides, hitting that costs drawing time and construction sequencing, and almost nothing in materials.

3. **The glazing problem is area, and the area is fixed.** Total window area is about
   <Var k="narrative.user_defined.window_area_total_ft2" /> ft<sup>2</sup>. The largest single orientation is the
   landmarked north front at <Var k="narrative.user_defined.window_area_north_ft2" /> ft<sup>2</sup>, which collects
   almost no useful winter sun and so runs at a net loss all season. That elevation cannot be reduced. What remains
   available is glazing quality and installation detailing.

4. **PHI EnerPHit by energy demand is the wrong target here.** The model reaches the 20 kWh/m<sup>2</sup>-yr
   heating-demand limit only at roughly R-60 on every assembly, including the interior face of the existing brick,
   with a 0.55 SHGC glazing package. On a 21 ft wide row house, R-60 of interior insulation eats a large share of the
   floor plate and drives the embedded joist ends colder and wetter. The high-SHGC glazing then works against summer
   comfort at the rear and penthouse glass. See
   [Passive House Certifications](/energy_model/#passive-house-certifications) for the alternative if a certificate is
   still wanted.

5. **Summer resilience is the one result that argues for a design change.** Under the Phius REVIVE seven-day summer
   outage, the modeled home does not stay habitable. See [Resilience](/resilience/) for the numbers and for what would
   move them.

#### What we recommend

- Adopt the "Recommended" variant as the design basis for the envelope and for schematic HVAC sizing.
- Carry <Var k="narrative.certification.ph_ach_limit" /> ACH50 as a contractual airtightness target, and test
  mid-construction rather than once at completion.
- Specify the window package at an installed U-value of <Var k="narrative.windows.comfort_target_u_value" />
  Btu/hr-ft<sup>2</sup>-F or better, a small step past the <Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" /> modeled
  here. That value clears the PHI thermal-comfort threshold for this climate, which is what lets perimeter heating
  come out.
- Treat the front elevation as a separate glazing problem, to be resolved against Landmarks in the envelope phase.
- Plan the fresh-air system as a dedicated ERV with its own ducting, sized for roughly
  <Var k="narrative.user_defined.design_airflow_cfm" /> cfm at design.
- Add fixed or operable shading at the rear and penthouse glazing. Confirm the summer resilience result before the
  glazing package is fixed.

This report covers Phase 01 of our scope. Assembly and hygrothermal design, thermal-bridge simulation, CD-phase model
updates, and NYCECC compliance support follow in later phases. Several recommendations here are written to carry into
that work without rework.
