#### Recommendation:

Target the PHI EnerPHit 'By Component' level of performance and adhere to the standard as a guide for the envelope, windows, thermal-bridging, air tightness and ventilation. While the building should use the EnerPHit framework as a guideline, we do **not** recommened pursuing actual PHI EnerPHit certification for this project. The inclusion of the masonry fireplaces makes certification impracticle in this case. Build the recommended package well, verify it with blower-door testing and commissioning, but skip the certification submission.

#### If a certificate is still wanted, PHI offers two routes

EnerPHit is PHI's retrofit standard, and it can be earned two ways. The **component method** sets a requirement for each assembly and ignores the whole-building demand figure. The **energy demand method** ignores the individual assemblies and sets a whole-building heating demand limit instead. A project satisfies one or the other, never a blend of the two. Both routes must additionally meet the general EnerPHit criteria for airtightness and renewable primary energy, and both are judged against this building's PHPP climate zone, Warm-temperate. Both variants shown throughout this report.

#### EnerPHit 'by Component'

This route sets a maximum U-value per assembly. Because this is a landmarked masonry building insulated from the
inside, the walls are judged against the interior-insulation limit, which is the more forgiving of the two. Roofs and
floor slabs are judged against the exterior-insulation limit even when the insulation sits inboard of the structure.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wall, interior insulation | R-<Var k="narrative.certification.enph_ag_int_limit" /> minimum | R-19.9 | meets |
| Roof | R-<Var k="narrative.certification.enph_ag_ext_limit" /> minimum | R-60.6 | meets |
| Floor slab and walls against ground | set by PHPP from this site's degree days | R-23 slab | meets |
| Windows, installed | U-<Var k="narrative.certification.enph_uw_limit" /> maximum | U-<Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" /> | meets |
| Airtightness | <Var k="narrative.certification.ph_ach_limit" /> ACH50 | <Var k="narrative.user_defined.rec_ach50" /> ACH50 | meets |
| Ventilation heat recovery | 75% minimum | <Var k="narrative.user_defined.erv_heat_recovery_pct" />% | meets |
| Renewable primary energy | 38,370 kWh/yr maximum | 31,430 kWh/yr | meets |

As modeled the package clears every line, which is why this variant carries the component-method name. The windows
were the last item to close: at an installed U-<Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" /> they now sit under both
the EnerPHit limit of U-<Var k="narrative.certification.enph_uw_limit" /> and the PHI comfort threshold of
U-<Var k="narrative.windows.comfort_target_u_value" />. The party wall drops out of the requirements because it faces
a conditioned neighbor.

Under this route the whole-building heating demand is **16,410 kWh/yr**, and no limit applies to it. Peak heating
load is about 35,000 Btu/h and peak sensible cooling about 15,600 Btu/h.

Two cautions on reading that as a pass. These are modeled design targets, not verified construction, and the
component method is judged on the as-built assemblies. The window number is an installed U-value, so it holds only
if the frame and the reveal detailing hold.

#### EnerPHit 'by Demand'

This route ignores the individual assemblies and asks instead that whole-building heating demand fall below
7,310 kWh/yr for this climate zone, with cooling and dehumidification demand under a combined limit PHPP sets from
the climate and the internal loads. The general airtightness and primary-energy criteria apply on top, as they do to
either route. Reaching the heating figure from a landmarked masonry shell with a large north-facing front elevation
takes a substantially deeper package:

| Component | Recommended package | Package required by demand method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exterior walls | R-19.9 | **R-60** |
| Walls against ground | R-19.9 | **R-60** |
| Party wall | R-<Var k="narrative.user_defined.rec_party_wall_r_value" /> | **R-60** |
| Basement slab | R-23 | **R-60** |
| Roof | R-60.6 | R-60.6 |
| Window SHGC | <Var k="narrative.user_defined.rec_window_shgc" /> | **0.55** |
| Windows, installed | U-<Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" /> | **U-0.16** |
| Thermal-bridge allowance | +10% | **+5%** |

The package clears the demand criteria, and the cost of clearing them is the point:

| Criterion | Limit | Result | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heating demand | 7,310 kWh/yr | 7,350 | meets |
| Cooling and dehumidification | 5,850 kWh/yr | 3,830 | meets |
| Renewable primary energy | 24,850 kWh/yr | 22,990 | meets |
| Airtightness | <Var k="narrative.certification.ph_ach_limit" /> ACH50 | 1.0 | meets |

Two things follow from that table. Heating demand lands at 7,350 kWh/yr against a limit of 7,310 kWh/yr, so the route is met with no margin at all: any as-built shortfall against these assemblies puts it back outside. And it is bought with roughly three times the wall insulation plus a party wall at R-60, which costs real floor area on every level, and with a 0.55 SHGC glazing package that runs directly against the 
<Var k="narrative.user_defined.rec_window_shgc" /> SHGC recommended for summer comfort in the
[Resilience](/resilience/) section. Total cooling demand rises 28% as a result. The demand route and the summer
overheating result pull in opposite directions on the same piece of glass.

<div class="btwr-callout">
  <strong>Why the component route is the one to keep open</strong>
  <span>
    The component route is met by the package we are already recommending, at no added cost and with no loss of floor
    area. The demand route requires a deeper, more expensive and less comfortable building, and it clears the heating
    target with no margin. If certification is pursued, it should be by component.
  </span>
</div>

<div class="btwr-callout">
  <strong>What this means for the decision</strong>
  <span>
    The certification decision can wait, and nothing in the recommended package forecloses it. If the owner wants to
    revisit certification at the end of design development, the added work is a certification-grade model, a longer
    thermal-bridge list, and the certifier submission. The building itself would barely change. We would want to run
    the full component-method check against the final assemblies before committing.
  </span>
</div>
