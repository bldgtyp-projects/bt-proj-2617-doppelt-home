We tested the building in five configurations. Each variant changes only the inputs listed, so the difference between
any two rows in the results below comes from those inputs alone.

1. **Code Minimum:** Meets the **<VarLink hrefKey="narrative.energy_code.code_base_url" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><Var
   k="narrative.energy_code.name" /> / <Var k="narrative.energy_code.zone" /></VarLink>** minimums for assembly
   U-factors, window U-values, airtightness and equipment efficiencies, and goes no further. The building has more
   than three stories above grade plane, so the Commercial Provisions of the code govern. This variant assumes a
   typical "extract-only" (bath fan) ventilation system, an uninsulated party wall, an uninsulated basement slab, and
   all-electric appliances and equipment for heating, cooling, hot water and cooking.

2. **Improved Envelope:** Adds interior insulation at the masonry walls (R-20), a fully insulated new basement slab
   (R-23), a partially insulated party wall (R-9), an improved roof (R-60), better glazing (U-0.24), and airtightness
   at 2.0 ACH50. Ventilation stays extract-only, so this variant isolates the envelope contribution.

3. **Improved Mech.:** Adds a dedicated balanced fresh-air ventilation system with heat recovery to the improved
   envelope. Heat recovery is modeled at 68% effective, representative of a mid-range unit. Fan energy rises slightly
   while heating demand falls.

4. **Recommended:** The package we recommend. It carries the improved envelope forward, tightens airtightness to
   <Var k="narrative.certification.ph_ach_limit" /> ACH50, upgrades the glazing to an installed U-value of
   <Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" /> Btu/hr-ft<sup>2</sup>-F at a
   <Var k="narrative.user_defined.rec_window_shgc" /> SHGC, uses a high-performance ERV at
   <Var k="narrative.user_defined.erv_heat_recovery_pct" />% effective heat recovery, and drops the thermal-bridge
   allowance to 10% on the strength of the detailing work scheduled in the next phase.

5. **EnerPHit by Demand:** Raises assembly R-values, window U-values and equipment efficiencies until the model meets
   the PHI EnerPHit energy-demand criterion of <Var k="narrative.certification.enph_hd_limit" /> kWh/m<sup>2</sup>-yr
   for heating. On this building that takes roughly R-60 at every assembly, including the interior face of the
   existing brick and the party wall, together with a 0.55 SHGC glazing package. It is here to price the
   certification target, and we do not recommend building it.

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <strong>Why the EnerPHit demand variant is priced but not recommended</strong>
  <span>
    R-60 of interior insulation on a 21 ft wide row house is roughly 15 in. of assembly depth against every exterior
    wall, which the floor plan cannot absorb. It also drives the embedded floor-joist ends colder and wetter, the
    exact failure mode we are trying to avoid on an interior-insulated masonry building. The 0.55 SHGC glazing needed
    to make the winter balance work then raises summer solar gain at the rear and penthouse glazing, where the model
    already shows the highest overheating risk.
  </span>
</div>
