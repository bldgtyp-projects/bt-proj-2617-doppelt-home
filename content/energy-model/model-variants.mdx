This assessment tests the building in five distinct variants. Each variant changes only the inputs listed, so the difference between any two rows in the results below comes from those inputs alone.

1. **Code Minimum:** Meets the **<VarLink hrefKey="narrative.energy_code.code_base_url" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><Var
   k="narrative.energy_code.name" /> / <Var k="narrative.energy_code.zone" /></VarLink>** minimums for assembly U-factors, window U-values, airtightness and equipment efficiencies, and goes no further. The building has more than three stories above grade plane, so the Commercial Provisions of the code govern. This variant assumes a typical "extract-only" (bath fan) ventilation system, an uninsulated party wall, an uninsulated basement slab, and all-electric appliances and equipment for heating, cooling, hot water and cooking.

2. **Improved Envelope:** This variant adds interior insulation at the masonry walls (R-20), a fully insulated new basement slab (R-23), a partially insulated party wall (R-9), an improved roof (R-60), better glazing (U-0.24), and airtightness at 2.0 ACH50. Ventilation stays extract-only, so this variant isolates the envelope contribution.

3. **Improved Mech.:** Adds a dedicated balanced fresh-air ventilation system with heat recovery to the improved
   envelope. Heat recovery is modeled at 68% effective, representative of a mid-range unit. Fan energy rises slightly
   while heating demand falls.

4. **EnerPHit 'By Component' (Recommended):** This version meets the prescriptive PHI EnerPHit (Energy Retrofit) standard. It carries the improved envelope forward, tightens
   airtightness to <Var k="narrative.certification.ph_ach_limit" /> ACH50, upgrades the glazing to an installed U-value of <Var k="narrative.windows.ph_window_u_value" /> Btu/hr-ft<sup>2</sup>-F at a <Var k="narrative.user_defined.rec_window_shgc" /> SHGC, uses a high-performance ERV at <Var k="narrative.user_defined.erv_heat_recovery_pct" />% effective heat recovery, and drops the thermal-bridge allowance to 10% on the strength of the detailing work scheduled in the next phase. Every assembly in it meets the PHI EnerPHit component limits for this climate zone, which is where the name comes from. See [Passive House Certifications](/energy_model/#passive-house-certifications).

5. **EnerPHit 'By Demand':** This version meets the performance-based PHI EnerPHit (Energy Retrofit) standard. It raises assembly R-values, window U-values and equipment efficiencies until the model meets the PHI EnerPHit energy-demand criterion for heating, cooling, and total primary (source) energy. On this building that takes roughly R-60 at every assembly, including the interior face of the existing brick and the party wall, together with a 0.55 SHGC glazing package. It is here to price the certification target, and we do not recommend building it.
