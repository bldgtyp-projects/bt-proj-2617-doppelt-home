"Site Energy" is the energy purchased by the building and delivered to the site by the utility. This is the most
typical energy use figure assessed when considering a site "Net-Zero" building energy balance, or the annual cost of
energy for the building. The site-energy total is made up of 6 main groups: heating, cooling, hot water, lighting,
electric equipment (appliances, electric vehicles, etc.), and pumps / fans.

All variants are modeled all-electric, with heat-pump heating, cooling and domestic hot water. Fuel-fired equipment is
permitted by code. Modeling it here would only obscure the envelope comparison, and the design intent for this project
is an all-electric house.

Two things to read off the chart below.

Heating dominates in the code-minimum case and falls fastest as the envelope improves. That is why envelope work
returns more per dollar than anything else on this project.

Hot water, lighting and appliances barely move between variants. Once heating is under control, those loads set the
floor on annual consumption. The levers left are equipment selection, occupant behavior, and on-site generation from
the penthouse-roof PV array shown on the drawing set.
