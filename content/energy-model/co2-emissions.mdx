Carbon dioxide and the other pollution that comes from burning fuel are the main driver of warming in the earth's
atmosphere and oceans. Limiting that warming means cutting CO<sub>2e</sub> (CO<sub>2</sub> equivalent) emissions from
buildings, industry and transportation. The specific target is debated. One widely used budget puts each person at
roughly 2.3 tons-CO<sub>2e</sub> per year by 2030 for all activities: about 1 ton for housing, 1 ton for
transportation, and 0.3 tons for food. A single US-to-Europe round trip flight releases about 4 tons on its own.
At an average annual occupancy of <Var k="building.total_num_occupants" /> people, the 1 ton per person housing
figure puts this building's target at under about <Var k="narrative.co2.target_tons" /> tons-CO<sub>2e</sub> per
year.

The modeled source energy and fuel types give an approximate annual CO<sub>2e</sub> figure. The totals below cover
fuel used for heating, cooling, hot water and all other plug loads. What each end use emits depends on how much fuel
it burns and which fuel it is (gas, electricity, etc.). Code permits fuel-fired heating, but we modeled every variant
with electric heat pumps only.

Output Emission Rates used are from the <strong><Var k="narrative.co2.epa_subgrid_name" /></strong> Subregion. For more information on these factors see 
the [EPA eGRID Data Explorer](https://www.epa.gov/egrid/data-explorer). Source Energy Factors for all fuel types are taken
from the [EPA EnergyStar Portfolio Manager Technical Reference (2023)](https://www.energystar.gov/buildings/tools-and-resources/portfolio-manager-technical-reference-source-energy).

The code-minimum variant lands near 12 tons-CO<sub>2e</sub> / year and the recommended package near 8.5
tons-CO<sub>2e</sub> / year, against the roughly <Var k="narrative.co2.target_tons" /> ton benchmark shown. No
envelope variant reaches the benchmark on operational emissions alone. Two things would close the remaining gap. The
penthouse-roof PV array shown on the drawing set is not yet in the model. The New York grid also keeps
decarbonizing, which is already the main reason an all-electric house is the right call here.
