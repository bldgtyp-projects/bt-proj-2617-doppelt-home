- **The penthouse is the hot spot.** Its glazing carries the highest summer radiation on the building by a wide
  margin. It sits above the neighboring rooflines with nothing shading it, it is heavily glazed on two sides, and it
  caps a full-height stair that feeds it warm air from every floor below. Shade it.
- **The fourth-floor terrace glazing is next.** Those doors and windows see high summer radiation and serve the
  primary bedroom suite, where night-time comfort matters most.
- **The rear extension glazing at the lower levels is moderate.** Neighboring construction across the rear yards
  shades the garden and parlor levels through most of the cooling season.
- **The street elevation is shaded all summer.** North-northwest orientation plus street trees leaves the front sash
  with almost no cooling load.

#### Recommendations

1. **Shade the penthouse from outside.** A fixed overhang, exterior blinds, or an operable exterior screen will all
   beat anything mounted inside the glass. The drawing set already shows a canopy over the penthouse roof. Develop it
   for solar control, not only for rain.
2. **Fit user-operable shading at the fourth-floor terrace glazing.** External is better. Internal blinds with high
   opacity and high solar reflectance work as a fallback and still cut glare.
3. **Specify a low SHGC at the rear and penthouse glazing.** The recommended package uses
   <Var k="narrative.user_defined.rec_window_shgc" /> throughout. Nothing in the winter results argues for going
   higher, and the summer results argue hard against it.
4. **Plan for stack effect.** A full-height stair with an occupied space at the top will stratify. Consider an
   operable vent or window at the penthouse that opens on summer nights, and coordinate it with the security and
   weather-tightness requirements.

The [Resilience](/resilience/) page takes the overheating question further, testing the building through a seven-day
summer power outage. That result is worse than the annual cooling numbers here suggest. Read it before the glazing
package is fixed.
