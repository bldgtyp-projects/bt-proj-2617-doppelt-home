The energy balance of the windows drives a high-performance building. The goal in this climate is to take some
advantage of wintertime solar gains to reduce heating energy need, while staying cautious about overheating and
cooling energy. Highly glazed rooms with large amounts of south- or west-facing glass make that balance harder.

All radiation values presented consider the local shading context. Where relevant, this context is created using
satellite images from Google Maps and plot lines from [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/#map=5/38.01/-95.84) and
[CadMapper](https://cadmapper.com/pro/login/?next=/pro/home). The site shading and orientation includes the following:

- A mid-block lot with attached neighbors on the east and adjoining construction to the west, so the two long
  elevations stay blocked for most of the year.
- The street elevation faces roughly north-northwest and the garden elevation roughly south-southeast, with the
  building rotated about 15° off the cardinal grid.
- Neighboring row houses of similar height on both sides, and taller construction across the rear yards, which shade
  the lower rear levels through much of the heating season.
- Street trees along West 11th Street. They shade the front elevation in summer and matter little in winter, since
  the front sees almost no direct sun in any season.

Only the upper rear levels, the fourth-floor terrace and the penthouse get meaningful direct sun. That one fact drives
most of the window conclusions in the two sections that follow.
