The results below give the average seasonal (winter / summer) solar radiation falling on the project's windows, taking
climate, orientation and shading into account. Radiation levels vary by orientation, and shading obstructions have a
strong effect on how much any individual window receives.

This is the most important result in the report, because it explains why the recommendation is what it is.

- **The glazing on this building loses far more than it gains.** Under the recommended package the windows lose
  roughly 8,750 kWh over the heating season and return roughly 2,600 kWh of solar gain. The net loss of about 6,150
  kWh is more than a third of the whole building's heating demand.
- **The front elevation contributes almost nothing.** It faces north-northwest, it is the largest single glazed
  orientation at <Var k="narrative.user_defined.window_area_north_ft2" /> ft<sup>2</sup>, and it returns roughly 550
  kWh over the whole heating season. It is also the elevation that cannot change, since it is landmarked. The only
  move available here is better glass.
- **The west-facing glazing returns almost nothing.** About
  <Var k="narrative.user_defined.window_area_west_ft2" /> ft<sup>2</sup> of glass, roughly a third of the total,
  returns under 60 kWh across the entire heating season, because the adjoining construction blocks it. If any glazing
  area gets reduced or reconsidered during design development, look here first.
- **The useful winter gain sits at the top of the rear.** The penthouse glazing, the fourth-floor terrace doors and
  the upper rear windows are the only openings receiving meaningful direct sun. Together they account for most of the
  south and east gain.

That distribution is why raising glazing SHGC does not rescue the winter balance. Pushing the solar-heat-gain
coefficient up across the package to chase an energy-demand target adds gain mostly where the sun already reaches, at
the top of the rear elevation, which is exactly where the summer results say to keep it out. The better response is to
attack the loss side: better installed U-values, a tighter envelope, and disciplined installation detailing at every
reveal.
