Below are our recommendations for the building envelope assemblies, based on the energy model results shown in
previous sections. They are recommendations only: the design team should review them and confirm they suit the
project.

The table summarizes the target performance for each assembly in the recommended package, alongside the code-minimum
baseline the model started from.

| Assembly | Code minimum | Recommended | Notes |
| --- | --: | --: | --- |
| Exterior wall, existing masonry | R-17.9 | **R-20** | Interior insulation, vapor-open to the inside |
| Foundation / basement wall | R-17.9 | **R-20** | New construction at the underpinning |
| Basement slab | R-1.8 | **R-23** | 4" continuous sub-slab |
| Exposed floor (rear extension, terrace) | R-30 | **R-30** | Already at target |
| Roof | R-38.5 | **R-60** | Cheapest R-value on the building |
| Party wall to neighbor | R-2 | **R-9** | Air sealing matters more than R-value |
| Windows, installed | U-0.27 | **U-0.17** | Clears the PHI comfort threshold and the EnerPHit component limit |
| Airtightness | 3.0 ACH50 | **1.0 ACH50** | See [Building Airtightness](#building-airtightness) |
| Thermal-bridge allowance | +20% | **+10%** | Requires the detailing work in the next phase |

**The walls stop at R-20 on purpose.** Every additional inch of interior insulation pushes the existing masonry and the embedded joist ends toward a colder, wetter winter condition. Around R-20 the added R-value the moisture risk on the masonry walls increases. We recommend sticking to an approximate R-20 for the walls in this case.

**The thermal-bridge allowance drops from 20% to 10%.** That reduction is challenging to acheive in practice. It assumes the detailing and simulation work scheduled for the next phase resolves the governing junctions: the joist-end penetrations, the slab
edge at the new underpinning, the party-wall returns, the window installation lines, and the parapet and terrace transitions. Those psi-values are also needed as code currency under NYCECC Section C402.1.4, so one set of simulations covers both.
