Below are our recommendations for the building envelope assemblies, based on the energy model results shown in
previous sections. These are recommendations only and should be reviewed by the design team to confirm they suit the
project.

The table summarizes the target performance for each assembly in the recommended package, alongside the code-minimum
baseline the model started from.

| Assembly | Code minimum | Recommended | Notes |
| --- | --: | --: | --- |
| Exterior wall, existing masonry | R-17.9 | **R-20** | Interior insulation, vapor-open to the inside |
| Foundation / basement wall | R-17.9 | **R-20** | New construction at the underpinning |
| Basement slab | R-1.8 | **R-23** | 6" continuous sub-slab |
| Exposed floor (rear extension, terrace) | R-30 | **R-30** | Already at target |
| Roof | R-38.5 | **R-60** | Cheapest R-value on the building |
| Party wall to neighbor | R-2 | **R-9** | Air sealing matters more than R-value |
| Windows, installed | U-0.27 | **U-0.19** | Also clears the PHI comfort threshold |
| Airtightness | 3.0 ACH50 | **1.0 ACH50** | See [Building Airtightness](#building-airtightness) |
| Thermal-bridge allowance | +20% | **+10%** | Requires the detailing work in the next phase |

Two decisions in that table need explaining, because they are where this building departs from a typical
new-construction Passive House project.

**The walls stop at R-20 on purpose.** Every additional inch of interior insulation on a 21 ft wide row house is an
inch of lost floor width across six levels, and it pushes the existing masonry and the embedded joist ends toward a
colder, wetter winter condition. Around R-20 the added R-value stops paying for the floor area and the moisture risk
it costs. The energy not recovered at the walls comes back at the roof, at the slab, and above all at the airtightness
target.

**The thermal-bridge allowance drops from 20% to 10%.** That reduction is not free. It assumes the detailing and
simulation work scheduled for the next phase resolves the governing junctions: the joist-end penetrations, the slab
edge at the new underpinning, the party-wall returns, the window installation lines, and the parapet and terrace
transitions. Those psi-values are also needed as code currency under NYCECC Section C402.1.4, so one set of
simulations covers both.
