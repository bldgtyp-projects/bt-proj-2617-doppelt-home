The model assumes a single high-performance ERV serving the whole house at
<Var k="narrative.user_defined.erv_heat_recovery_pct" />% effective heat recovery. The
<VarLink hrefKey="narrative.mechanical.erv.link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><Var k="narrative.mechanical.erv.manufacturer_name" />{" "}<Var k="narrative.mechanical.erv.type_name" /></VarLink>
fits this project. These units ship with MERV 13 filtration by default, which matters on a Manhattan street.

Design airflow is approximately <Var k="narrative.user_defined.design_airflow_cfm" /> cfm at the maximum setting and
roughly <Var k="narrative.user_defined.standard_airflow_cfm" /> cfm in normal operation. That puts the design flow
near the top of this unit's range, and the next size down in the ComfoAir Q line does not reach it. Two conditions
specific to this building make the remaining headroom worth keeping. A six-level row house needs long vertical duct
runs, and rated airflow falls as external static pressure rises, so the pressure loss binds before the nominal
capacity does. A unit running below its limit is also quieter and more efficient than one running at it.

Use an ERV rather than an HRV here. New York summers carry enough latent load that recovering moisture on the exhaust
side earns its keep, and the summer resilience results on the [Resilience](/resilience/) page make the same argument.

The values used in this analysis are assumed only. Contact
<VarLink hrefKey="narrative.mechanical.erv.link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><Var k="narrative.mechanical.erv.manufacturer_name" /></VarLink>
directly for unit selection and sizing. Give them the room-by-room airflows from
[Fresh-Air Flow Rates](#fresh-air-flow-rates) and the riser layout, and have them confirm the model, the duct sizing
and the external static pressure this design imposes. Retain them to design, specify and balance the system after
installation. Every high-performance building needs this step. Zehnder America packages normally include balancing,
but verify it in this case.

#### System balancing

Test and balance the fresh-air system before occupancy, to confirm good air mixing and adequate supply to all living
spaces. The vendor should provide this as part of their services. Measured supply and extract totals should land
within 10% of each other.

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <strong>Space planning</strong>
  <span>
    Allocate the ERV location, its filter access, its condensate drain, and its riser path now. On a narrow row house
    the vertical chase is the scarce resource, and the fresh-air ducting cannot share it with the heating and cooling
    ducting. Both systems need their own space.
