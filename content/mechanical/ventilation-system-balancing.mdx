The model assumes a single high-performance ERV serving the whole house at
<Var k="narrative.user_defined.erv_heat_recovery_pct" />% effective heat recovery. The
<VarLink hrefKey="narrative.mechanical.erv.link" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><Var k="narrative.mechanical.erv.manufacturer_name" /> <Var k="narrative.mechanical.erv.type_name" /></VarLink>
fits this project. These units ship with MERV 13 filtration by default, which is what keeps the incoming air clean on
a Manhattan street.

Design airflow is approximately <Var k="narrative.user_defined.design_airflow_cfm" /> cfm. A smaller unit could
deliver that on paper. Stay with the larger unit for two reasons specific to this building. A seven-level row house
needs long vertical duct runs, so external static pressure binds before airflow does. And a unit running at a lower
fraction of its capacity is quieter and more efficient than a small one running near its limit.

Use an ERV rather than an HRV here. New York summers carry enough latent load that recovering moisture on the exhaust
side earns its keep, and the summer resilience results on the [Resilience](/resilience/) page make the same argument.

The values used in this analysis are assumed only. Retain the ventilation vendor to design, specify and balance the
system after installation. Every high-performance building needs this step. Zehnder America packages normally include
balancing, but verify it in this case.

#### System balancing

Test and balance the fresh-air system before occupancy, to confirm good air mixing and adequate supply to all living
spaces. The vendor should provide this as part of their services. Measured supply and extract totals should land
within 10% of each other.

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <strong>Space planning</strong>
  <span>
    Allocate the ERV location, its filter access, its condensate drain, and its riser path now. On a narrow row house
    the vertical chase is the scarce resource, and the fresh-air ducting cannot share it with the heating and cooling
    ducting. Both systems need their own space.
  </span>
</div>
