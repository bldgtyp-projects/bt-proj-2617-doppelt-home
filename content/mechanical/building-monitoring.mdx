#### Environmental monitoring

Several systems track temperature, RH, CO2 and other indoor conditions. Install one. The hardware is cheap, and it is
how the team commissions the home and tracks down comfort problems over the first year. Two options we would consider: the
**Wireless Sensor Tag** by Cao Gadgets LLC, and the **Netatmo** weather station. Both upload data over a wireless
network and both are readable online, so the home needs a wireless network running at all times. Newer platforms such
as **Ecobee** also work.

The Wireless Tag system costs less but cannot monitor CO2, so we would take the Netatmo.

More information can be found at:

- [Ecobee](https://www.ecobee.com/)
- [WirelessTag](https://wirelesstag.net/)
- [Netatmo](https://www.netatmo.com/)

#### Energy monitoring

A branch-circuit monitoring system is the only way to see where a home's energy actually goes. Watching each circuit
over the first months of operation catches equipment that is running wrong and drawing more than it should. This
project has a new electrical service, so a load center built for the job makes sense. Several manufacturers offer load
centers with integrated branch-circuit monitoring that tie into other smart controls such as switching and dimming.
The product app carries the real-time and historical energy-use data.

More information can be found at:

- [Leviton](https://leviton.com/products/residential/load-centers/the-leviton-smart-load-center)
- [Span](https://www.span.io/)

Other options bolt onto a conventional circuit breaker panel. The Curb energy monitor, the Emporia Vue and the eGauge
all monitor electrical usage in real time, with a web or app interface that breaks down where the energy went.

More information can be found at:

- [EnergyCurb](https://poweredbyelevation.com/curb-energy-monitoring/)
- [Emporia Energy](https://www.emporiaenergy.com/)
- [eGauge](https://www.egauge.net/)
