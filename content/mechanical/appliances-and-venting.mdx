#### Kitchen venting

Vent the kitchen at the lowest rate that satisfies regulations. For gas cooking, code sets the flow rate. For electric
cooking, hold exhaust hood airflow to 200 to 400 cfm maximum by picking a correctly sized hood. The Home Ventilation
Institute (HVI) specifies a minimum of 40 cfm and recommends 100 cfm per linear foot of range. Hood sizing and
placement matter more than airflow rate, because they set capture efficiency. Mount hoods as low as practical, extend
them beyond the range by about six inches at the sides, and make them as deep as the range will allow.

#### Makeup air systems

Venting exhaust appliances (clothes dryers, kitchen hoods, etc.) in an airtight home may call for a dedicated makeup
air system. That goes double for homes with indoor combustion appliances such as wood stoves and fireplaces, where an
automatic makeup air fan balances the exhausted air.

We do NOT recommend this strategy in most cases. It is expensive, it complicates the insulation and air-sealing
detailing, and it costs energy to heat and cool the makeup air.

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <span>
    Use all-electric systems wherever possible, and install combustion appliances only in outdoor locations (porches,
    decks, etc.).
  </span>
</div>

Outdoor makeup air may need heating or cooling once it enters the habitable space. Include self-closing (magnetic)
dampers on all ducting to stop air infiltration and exfiltration when the appliances are off. To hold comfort while
makeup air is being supplied, size and install a high-output heating element to bring the incoming air up to
temperature.

Two self-modulating makeup air systems (MUAS) with matching makeup air heaters (MUAH) would suit this project:

- [Electro Industries](https://electromn.com/make-up-air-2/)
- [Neptronic](https://www.neptronic.com/cmu/)

#### Makeup air system commissioning

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <span>
    The home is only safe to operate if the makeup air system is commissioned properly and maintained. With
    combustion appliances present, this is a life-safety item.
  </span>
</div>

When configuring such a system:

1. Ensure airtightness at all envelope penetrations.
2. Insulate all ducting with vapor-closed insulation (min 2") and vapor-closed tape at all joints.
3. Provide no more than 60% of the make-up air at the base of the range/oven.
4. For gas cooking, follow all local code requirements and the manufacturer's instructions.
